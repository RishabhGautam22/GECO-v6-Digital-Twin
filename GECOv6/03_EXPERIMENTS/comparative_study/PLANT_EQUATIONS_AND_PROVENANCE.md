# Independent Hovorka plant and original-family comparator

The new Hovorka implementation follows R. Hovorka et al., “Nonlinear model predictive control of glucose concentration in subjects with type1 diabetes,” *Physiological Measurement*, vol.25, pp.905-920,2004, doi:[10.1088/0967-3334/25/4/010](https://doi.org/10.1088/0967-3334/25/4/010), equations1-7 and Tables1-2. The research agent visually verified Table2 in the supplied original PDF; its page909 rendering is `01_RESEARCH_PROTOCOL/literature_editorial/Hovorka2004_page909.png`. The publisher's original paper, not third-party implementation code, is the equation source.

With accessible and remote glucose amounts Q1,Q2 in mmol; subcutaneous insulin S1,S2 in mU; plasma insulin I in mU/L; insulin effects x1,x2 in min⁻¹ and x3 dimensionless:

```
G = Q1/VG
dQ1 = -F01c - x1*Q1 + k12*Q2 - FR + UG + EGP0*(1-x3)
dQ2 = x1*Q1 - (k12+x2)*Q2
dS1 = u - S1/tmaxI
dS2 = (S1-S2)/tmaxI
dI = S2/(tmaxI*VI) - ke*I
dxi = kai*(SIi*I-xi), i=1,2,3
F01c = F01*min(1,max(G,0)/4.5)
FR = 0.003*max(G-9,0)*VG
dD1 = AG*carbohydrate_rate_mmol - D1/tmaxG
dD2 = (D1-D2)/tmaxG
UG = D2/tmaxG
```

The two gut states implement the paper's impulse-response meal equation for arbitrary meal inputs. Inputs are constant rates over each five-minute bin. Insulin research U are converted to1000mU; carbohydrate grams to mmol with0.1801559g/mmol; mmol/L glucose to mg/dL with18.01559. The sensor adds a separately labelled9-min interstitial lag to provide the same type of observation as the original-family model; this sensor equation is a study extension, not an equation attributed to Hovorka.

Nominal constants are k12=.066, ka1=.006, ka2=.06, ka3=.03 and ke=.138min⁻¹; VI=.12L/kg, VG=.16L/kg; AG=.8; tmaxG=40min, tmaxI=55min; EGP0=.0161 and F01=.0097mmol/kg/min. Table2 explicitly gives SI values51.2×10⁻⁴,8.2×10⁻⁴ and520×10⁻⁴ (the first two min⁻¹ per mU/L, the third per mU/L). These are prior means in that paper, not individually identified synthetic-patient values. Body weight and prespecified factor perturbations are generated by this study; no patient population distribution is claimed.

The code solves the nonnegative fasting equilibrium for basal insulin at120mg/dL and integrates with RK4 at one-minute substeps. Tests check equilibrium preservation,1000mU/U scaling, gut-chain impulse response, insulin-effect direction and convergence against half-minute RK4. The published EGP term is not silently clipped to nonnegative production. Negative glucose compartments or other out-of-domain states are retained and flagged; any such results limit interpretation of glucose-band outcomes. F01c is zero below zero glucose to avoid a negative consumption artifact, but this does not render negative glucose physiological.

The comparison plant uses the production `GlucoseModel` with its original five-state structure and prespecified synthetic parameter perturbations. Its existing20-600mg/dL clipping is retained, with boundary samples flagged. The two plants are structurally different (one versus two glucose compartments, one versus three insulin-action states, direct versus two-stage subcutaneous insulin, one versus two gut transit states). Neither constitutes an independently validated patient simulator for this study.

The trace field `plant_valid` is only a numerical/domain screen; it is not physiological validation. Hovorka x3>1 is separately counted as negative endogenous production and fails that screen even when glucose remains positive. The equation remains unchanged. Original-family plasma/interstitial/action clamp flags are reported separately, because numerical boundedness can conceal clipping-dependent behavior.
